01

Overview

A small tool that runs inside AutoCAD and logs whose time goes to which job, automatically, while you draw. This page is the reference for using it — not for installing it.

Open a drawing, tell it the job once, and go. It watches for activity the same way AutoCAD does — commands, edits, saves — pauses itself when you step away, and banks the time when you close the drawing. Nothing to remember to start or stop under normal use.

Every session lands in a plain, searchable record — by job, by person, by day or week — that your CAD manager can pull up without asking anyone what they worked on.

Job numbers

P + 5 digits

e.g. P10432. Typed once, remembered per drawing and per job after that.

Idle rule

5 min

Step away longer than that and the clock credits five minutes, then stops until you're back.

Multiple drawings

Independent

Only the drawing you're actually working in accrues time — the rest sit paused.

Never guesses

Asks first

A template or a new blank drawing is never silently billed to whatever job you had open before.

Worth knowing up front

You will occasionally see a one-line message on the command line telling you what it just did — which job it started tracking, or that a drawing is untracked until you set one. That line is the whole interface outside of the pop-up; it's worth actually reading it once in a while.

02

Daily Workflow

What actually happens, in order, from opening a drawing to closing it. Steps marked automatic need nothing from you.

1

Open a drawing

AutoCAD loads as normal. The tracker starts in the background — you won't notice unless it needs something from you.

2

The pop-up appears (usually)

Confirm the job number, pick the project manager, add a task or a note if you like, click OK. It's pre-filled whenever the drawing itself gives it something to go on — see Sheet 03.

3

Work normallyautomatic

Drawing, editing, saving — any of it counts as active time. There's no timer to watch and nothing to click to keep it running.

4

Step awayautomatic

Longer than five minutes of no activity pauses the clock. See Sheet 04 for exactly how much of that gap still gets billed.

5

Come backautomatic

The next thing you do resumes tracking. Nothing to reset or restart.

6

Close the drawing

The session is banked to the record the moment it closes. If it's your only open drawing and the total was under about ten seconds, it isn't recorded at all — opening a file just to glance at it doesn't clutter anyone's timesheet.

Needing to stop without closing

Heading into a long meeting, or switching to something the drawing's job shouldn't be charged for? Type CHSTOP — it banks the time and stops the clock. CHSTART picks it back up.

04

How Time Is Tracked

There's no visible stopwatch. Time is worked out from what you actually did — and the idle rule is the one piece of math worth understanding, so try it below.

Add some time above to see how each stretch gets billed.

The rule behind that demo: a gap of five minutes or less since your last action is billed in full — that covers looking something up, answering a phone call at your desk, thinking through a detail. A gap longer than five minutes bills the first five and writes off the rest, the moment you come back and do something.

More than one drawing open

Each open drawing is tracked on its own. Switch tabs and the one you were in pauses; the one you switch to picks up. You can never accidentally double-bill the same stretch of time to two jobs at once.
